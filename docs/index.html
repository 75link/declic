Why I built it

Shooting a cycling race means holding the shutter down. At 11 frames a second, one rider going past is twenty frames, and only one of them has the wheel, the face and the light exactly right. That's how you get the shot.

After two days at a race I came home with 3,622 RAW files on one card. Finding that one frame in every burst is the slowest part of the job, and it's exactly the part a computer is good at: comparing frames taken a tenth of a second apart and checking which one is sharpest on the rider.

So I built a tool that does that first pass on my own laptop, without uploading anything, and leaves the final call to me. That turned into Declic.

What it actually does

Point it at a folder or plug in your card. It reads everything and stores what it learns in a local database. Your originals stay untouched unless you tell it to move something.

Groups your bursts

Frames shot seconds apart that show the same moment end up together, ranked from best to worst. Each one comes with a short reason, so you can disagree with it.

Judges focus on the subject

It finds the rider, the runner, the bird, and checks how sharp they are. A busy sharp background won't win over a sharp face.

Catches duplicates

Exact copies, the JPEG you exported last year next to its RAW, three versions of the same edit. The original is kept as the master.

Knows who's in the photo

Faces are grouped into people. Give someone a name once and you can pull up every photo of them.

Search by describing

Type "crowd cheering at the finish" or "bike close-up" and it finds them. No tagging by hand.

Shows the space you'd get back

The dashboard tells you how much of your card is weaker frames and duplicates before you touch anything.

It doesn't punish good technique

Most "blur detectors" look at the whole picture. That's how a culling tool ends up throwing away your best panning shot. Declic measures the subject separately, checks your shutter speed, and looks at which way the blur goes.

Panning

Motion blur streaks in one direction. Lens blur doesn't. On my test card, fast-shutter backgrounds scored around 1.2 on that measure and 1/60 s pans went up to 12, so it can tell them apart.

Shallow depth of field

A soft background at f/2.8 is a choice, not a mistake. Focus is scored on the person, never on what's behind them.

Silhouettes and low-key light

Dark on purpose isn't underexposed. A local vision model looks at the photo with your camera settings and flags deliberate choices so they don't get marked down.

Your photos stay on your Mac

Every model runs locally on Apple Silicon. After the one-time model download, Declic works with the Wi-Fi off.

  • No account, no cloud, no upload.
  • No analytics or tracking in the app. This page doesn't load any third-party scripts or fonts either.
  • It never deletes anything. Rejects you approve get moved to a staging folder on the same drive, and one click puts them all back.
  • RAW files are read, never written to.

Where it's at

Declic is early. It works well on my own photos, which isn't the same as working well on yours. Here's the honest list.

Works today
  • Sony ARW, plus JPEG, HEIC and TIFF. Canon, Nikon, Fuji and DNG go through the same macOS decoder but haven't been tested yet.
  • Burst grouping with ranked frames and reasons
  • Subject focus, panning and depth-of-field awareness
  • Duplicate and export detection
  • People, search, storage dashboard
  • Review, approve, stage, undo
Coming next

  • Testing on more cameras. So far it's mostly been Sony ARW.
  • Learning from your own keep and reject choices

Questions

Will it delete my photos?

No. Declic doesn't have a delete button. It can move rejects you approved into a staging folder on the same drive, and undo puts every file back where it was. Emptying that folder is up to you.

Does it replace Lightroom?

No. It's for the part before editing: going from a full card to the frames worth opening in Lightroom or Capture One.

What do I need?

A Mac with Apple Silicon. I've been running it on an M1 Max with 32 GB of memory. The models take about 5.5 GB of disk.

Windows?

Not for now. The vision model runs on Apple's MLX, and I'd rather make one platform good first.

Will it be free?

It's free during the beta. I haven't decided what happens after. What I can promise is that it won't become a subscription you need to keep your own ratings, and your data will stay in files you own.

Where's the code?

Not public yet. I want it to install cleanly on someone else's Mac before I put it out there.

Try it on your next shoot

I'm looking for a small group of photographers who shoot a lot of bursts: sports, events, wildlife, kids. You'd get early builds, I'd get to see where it gets things wrong.
